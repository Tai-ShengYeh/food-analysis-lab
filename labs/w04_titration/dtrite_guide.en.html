DLAB dTrite Digital Titrator User Guide

How to use the DLAB dTrite electronic bottle-top titrator

🎯 Students at the bench: Sections 1, 4, 5🧰 TA before class: Sections 2, 3, 6, 9 📘 Sources: manufacturer manual Ver.20211210, manufacturer demo video

中文版 · Companion lab: Glass burette vs digital burette · To print: browser Print → A4.

In one sentence: the dTrite is a burette that mounts on the reagent bottle and uses a motor to push a piston and dispense liquid. You still judge the endpoint by eye from the phenolphthalein color; it only replaces "reading the scale" with "reading the screen" and is not an automatic potentiometric titrator.

1. Know the controller: 4 buttons + 1 knob

The set has three parts: the main unit (barrel and valves) that mounts on the bottle, the controller with the screen, and the magnetic stirrer that clips onto the side of the controller. A remote handle is also supplied (same functions as the knob, cable about 90 cm).

Most common mistakeA short press on the knob = volume reset to zero. At the endpoint, copy the number on the screen first, then touch the knob. During dropwise addition only "turn" the knob; do not "press" it.

How to read the screen

  • Large number: the cumulative volume dispensed since the last reset (mL), shown to two decimal places. At 99.99 mL it stops automatically; press the knob to reset.
  • ▲ / ▼ arrow: the direction the piston is moving.
  • st✱: the stirrer is running. The top-right corner shows the battery level.

Return valve (the small knob on the side of the main unit)

Titrate position (marked ① in the manual)
Liquid is dispensed from the titrating pipe tip. Keep it here at all times.

Return position (marked ② in the manual)
Liquid goes back to the reagent bottle; use it only when recovering reagent and cleaning at the end of class. The video turns the valve so it is perpendicular to the tube.

The valve orientation icons in the scanned manual are unclear; the TA should confirm on this unit and attach a label (see Section 9).

2. First-time assembly (TA)

For a new unit, or one that has been in storage for a long time, check both valves before assembly. Video 1:27

  1. Check the balls in the valves: use the supplied tool to gently poke the dispensing valve and the filling valve, and confirm the ball inside moves slightly (a stuck ball means no fill or no dispense).
  2. Fit the titrating pipe: press the end of the titrating pipe into the locating slot on the main unit and push it all the way in, connect it to the dispensing valve and tighten; put the protective cap on the tip.
  3. Fit the filling pipe: connect it to the filling valve; the length should reach near the bottom of the bottle without touching it.
  4. Mount on the bottle: from the 5 bottle adapters (GL25, GL28, GL32, GL38, S40) choose the one that fits the NaOH bottle; screw it onto the main unit first, then onto the bottle. Check that the adapter is tight before every use; do not twist the main unit during operation.
  5. Connect the controller and stirrer: clip the stirrer into the slot on the side of the controller and turn it to horizontal until it locks, then plug the stirrer's USB into the controller; connect the main unit to the controller with the USB cable.
  6. Power on: power switch on the back of the controller ("O" = off, "–" = on); wait for the self-test to finish. The panel can tilt back up to 45°.
  7. Turn the return valve to the titrate position, then do the Section 3 pre-fill (purge).

Back of the controller, left to right: power, remote handle socket, communication port (no function), charge / communication port, main unit socket. The titrating pipe is FEP; check compatibility in Section 8 before changing reagents.

3. Before every use: purge air bubbles + set speeds (TA)

When to purge: a new unit, unused for more than a week, or after the bottle or titrating pipe has been removed. Video 3:03

  1. Place a waste beaker under the tip (purging dispenses liquid).
  2. Long press ⟲ for about 2 s; the piston automatically fills and dispenses one cycle.
  3. Repeat 3–5 times until no bubbles are visible in the barrel and titrating pipe. One or two tiny bubbles are fine; for large bubbles purge a few more times, and if that does not work contact the vendor.
  4. If a drop hangs from the tip, touch it off gently against the wall of the waste beaker.

Set the speeds Video 3:27

Long press ⚙ for about 2 s to enter setup → short press ⚙ to change page → turn the knob to adjust, press the knob to confirm → on any page, long press ⚙ for about 2 s to leave.

4. Titrate one sample (students)

The manufacturer video demonstrates HCl + methyl orange (red → yellow); this course uses NaOH titrating white vinegar / juice + phenolphthalein (colorless → pale pink); the button operations are the same. Video 4:37

  1. Place the flask and start stirring: put the Erlenmeyer flask (sample + phenolphthalein) on the stirrer, drop in a stir bar (20 mm maximum), and short press ⚙ to start stirring. Set the speed so nothing splashes out.
  2. Fill first: hold ▲ (or long press the knob for 2 s) to fill the barrel. Fill it completely before each sample; avoid having to refill halfway through a titration.
  3. Align and zero: position the tip just inside the mouth of the flask, short press the knob, and the screen shows 0.00 mL.
  4. Fast stage: hold ▼ to dispense. When the pink color appears and fades more and more slowly, release (about 1 mL before the estimated endpoint).
  5. Dropwise stage: switch to turning the knob one notch at a time, slower as you get closer, until the pale pink persists for 30 s.
  6. Record: the number on the screen is the total NaOH used for this sample. Copy it to the record sheet first (to 0.01 mL), then do anything else.
  7. Next sample: short press ⚙ to stop stirring, change the flask, and return to step 1.

NaOH runs out mid-titrationRelease all buttons, hold ▲ to refill, then continue. Whether the cumulative number on the screen carries on or jumps after a refill must be tested by the TA before class (Section 9); until confirmed, record the numbers both before and after the refill.

5. End of class: recover reagent and clean

Return the NaOH in the barrel to the bottle Video 5:55

  1. Turn the return valve to the return position.
  2. Hold ▼ until the piston reaches the end and the barrel is empty (liquid flows back into the bottle, not out of the tip).
  3. Turn the return valve back to the titrate position.

Clean the barrel (at the end of the course, or when changing reagent)

  1. Move the main unit onto a bottle of deionized water (or put the filling pipe into water), with the return valve at the titrate position.
  2. Fill with ▲ and dispense with ▼, repeating at least 5 times, collecting the water in the waste beaker.
  3. Long press ⟲ to pre-fill (purge) once.
  4. Lift the filling pipe out of the water and hold ▲ to draw air all the way; turn the return valve to the return position and hold ▼ to push out the residual water; turn the valve back to the titrate position.
  5. The housing is ABS plastic; wipe it with clean water. Do not autoclave.
  • Do not leave NaOH in the barrel overnight (course rule): dried crystals make the piston stick (the manual's troubleshooting lists "crystals and precipitates" as a cause of a rough piston).
  • Students must not take apart the barrel or valves; if it cannot be cleaned, ask the TA to contact the distributor. Empty it before sending it for repair, and tell them what reagent was last used.
  • Storage: 0–40 °C, humidity below 80%. If unused for a long time, charge it once a month and keep the battery above 50%.

Reading this table: at all three volumes the unit actually delivers more than the set value. At 1 mL the difference is about 1%, and from 5 mL up it falls below 0.1%: on the same instrument, the smaller the volume delivered, the larger the relative error. So design the titration so the endpoint volume is 5 mL or more; Part A uses 10 mL for the gravimetric verification, which falls right in the low-error range.

Calibration (rewriting the calibration values in the instrument) needs the manufacturer's dedicated software, a 0.01 mg balance and a 20–25 °C constant-temperature room, and is done by the distributor. The weighing we do in Part A is a verification, not a calibration.

8. Chemical compatibility

The wetted parts are borosilicate glass, FEP and PTFE. The manual lists the usable reagents (maximum concentration 1 mol/L); the NaOH, HCl and acetic acid used in this course are all in the table, along with KOH, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, EDTA, Na₂S₂O₃ and others. AgNO₃, KMnO₄, iodine solution and others are marked "*", which the manual does not explain; ask the distributor before using them.

  • Do not use: hydrofluoric acid; cloudy liquids with solid particles (e.g. activated-carbon suspensions, which clog it); carbon disulfide.
  • Will swell the plastic parts: concentrated acids and bases, and non-polar organic solvents such as benzene and toluene.
  • Do not keep it in an environment with corrosive gases such as HCl fumes.

9. TA pre-lab checklist

The points below are not clearly explained in the manual and video, or the two contradict each other. Try them on this unit and write the results on this handout:

  • Return valve: which position is titrate and which is return (video: perpendicular to the tube = return)? Attach labels on the main unit.
  • Barrel capacity per fill: after filling completely and dispensing until it stops, how many mL does the screen show? ____ mL
  • Refill mid-titration: dispense halfway, press ▲ to refill, then dispense again: does the cumulative number carry on? ____ (a KHP standardization uses about 25 mL, so this will definitely come up)
  • Volume per knob notch: turn one notch and watch the screen: is it 0.01 mL or 0.1 mL? ____ (compare with the "increment 100 µL" wording in the spec table; the Part A estimate assumes 0.01 mL)
  • Button hold time: does it trigger after a long press of about 2 s (manual) or 3 s (video)?
  • NaOH bottle thread: which adapter fits? ____
  • Battery: charge fully before class (about 4 h).
  • Stirrer: setup page III only has an effect once it is connected; prepare stir bars of 20 mm or smaller.

Part A reminder: the manual has no "preset dispense 10.00 mL" mode; students dispense by hand with ▼ / the knob to close to 10 mL and simply record the volume actually shown on the screen; it does not need to be exactly 10.00.

Where the video and the manual disagree (the manual takes precedence)

  • Long-press time: the video says 3 s; the manual says about 2 s.
  • The caption at 5:01 in the video says "hold the bottom-right button to dispense quickly", but the Dispense button is at bottom left (▼); the same video also says bottom left is Dispense at 4:14.
