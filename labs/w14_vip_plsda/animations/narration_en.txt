Before trusting a wavelength ranking, verify the calculation. This week starts with an extra-sum VIP bug, then explores classification and two complementary outlier diagnostics. All three datasets are the original NB12 teaching simulations, not real inspection results.

Each latent variable has a vector of wavelength weights. VIP combines their squared contributions, weighted by explained response variation. Correct VIP has mean squared value one across all variables, but that identity need not hold for a small selected subset.

This example has eighty-one variables. Prematurely summing the wavelength contributions makes every buggy VIP equal nine, with mean square eighty-one. But filling every VIP with one would pass the mean-square check. Also inspect the curve, implementation, and scientific context.

VIP is nonnegative importance, not causality or a direction of concentration change. Regression coefficients carry signs, interpreted in the context of preprocessing and collinearity. VIP above one is a common above-average heuristic, not a significance test.

The demo selects eighteen VIP variables from sixty training samples, with twenty samples held out. Selecting on all training data before cross-validation lets validation folds influence selection. Reselect within each fold, or evaluate once on untouched external samples.

Encode six oil classes as six columns of zeros and ones. PLS jointly regresses those targets. For a new sample, choose the class with the largest predicted score. These scores are not calibrated probabilities: they can be negative or exceed one.

Refined olive oil has only two true positives out of four: sensitivity one half. Perfect specificity does not repair those missed positives.

T squared scales score distance by each retained component's variance. Q is the squared reconstruction residual, measuring variation outside the retained model. Both use the same calibration PCA, but answer different questions. Inspect them together.

Fit a two-component PCA using only forty calibration samples. The leverage example has T squared about twenty-eight point four but Q below its limit. Simulated instrument-shift samples have ordinary T squared yet Q about thirty-three times its limit. An ordinary score plot does not establish applicability.

Limits belong to the fitted calibration model, not every instrument or dataset.
